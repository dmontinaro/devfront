Vision Paper · Working Draft

devfront.dev

A world-builder powered by real development work: missions, skills, guilds, and visible progress, built as an open-source trust anchor.

Not “Jira with XP.” Not a coding game. Not play-to-earn. The fantasy is not to click a fictional city into existence, but to shape a living technical world through meaningful work.

Real work → visible progress → better missions → a richer world.
MVP: one personal starting world Later: regions, worlds, and connected universes GitHub-verified + private missions Open-source development

1. Core Thesis

devfront makes productive development work emotionally visible. The primary reward is not status, money, or a badge. It is the feeling that my real work is shaping a world of my own.

Primary Reward

Your world visibly evolves. Its places, systems, history, and capabilities reflect what you have actually built.

Supporting Rewards

Skill growth and shared guild worlds create depth and long-term motivation.

Useful Side Effects

Mission discovery is utility. Reputation and status emerge from meaningful, verified work.

3. World Model

The MVP begins with one personal starting world. It is broader than a settlement: work can create landscapes, structures, infrastructure, ecosystems, landmarks, routes, archives, and social connections.

1 · SeedA personal world with an initial identity and purpose.
2 · DomainsBuild, Stability, Knowledge, Trust, and Automation shape the world differently.
3 · RegionsProjects and capabilities become distinct places and systems.
4 · WorldsRepositories, communities, teams, and themes become explorable worlds.
5 · UniversesPublic and private worlds can connect without losing their boundaries.

4. Work Input

devfront must represent more than open-source work while preserving trust. GitHub-verified work is the gold standard, but private missions remain possible and visibly distinct.

Gold Progress

Merged PRs, closed issues, approved reviews, passing tests, deployments, and CI signals. Publicly verifiable and weighted more strongly.

Private Progress

Manual missions for client work, internal projects, learning, or agent workflows. They shape the world but carry a different evidence marker.

Guided Progress

Recommended missions based on skills, stack, goals, world state, and optionally GitHub history.

5. Mission System

An MVP mission is a real unit of work lasting roughly one to four hours. Larger efforts become campaigns composed of multiple missions.

TypeScopeExamplesReward Principle
Small Mission15–60 minutesFix documentation, reproduce a bug, triage an issueSmall XP and resources, quick momentum
Standard Mission1–4 hoursBug fix, test, feature slice, UI improvementThe MVP default: meaningful progress without overhead
Major Mission0.5–2 daysIntegration, module refactor, larger featureMore XP, stronger verification, higher impact
CampaignMultiple missionsStabilize checkout, build an AI review pipelineCompletion bonus and larger world transformation

Design rule: rewards derive from scope × difficulty × importance × verification × quality × impact, not time alone.

6. World-Building System

Every completed mission creates three outcomes: skill XP, resources, and a visible transformation of the world.

Build

Code, features, UI, and product work can create structures, production systems, and developed regions.

Stability

Tests, bug fixes, QA, security, and reliability can create shields, resilient infrastructure, and protected zones.

Knowledge

Documentation, research, and architecture can create archives, observatories, maps, and discovered territory.

Trust

Reviews, mentoring, and collaboration can create routes, embassies, guild spaces, and stronger relationships.

Automation

Agents, DevOps, CI/CD, and tooling can create autonomous systems, energy networks, and machine ecosystems.

Example

Writing an end-to-end test increases Testing XP and Stability, then visibly fortifies the affected region.

7. UX Principle

The world is the center. The mission board is not the product home; it supplies meaningful paths toward the next transformation.

8. Onboarding

The MVP starts quickly through self-selection. GitHub analysis arrives later as a magic moment, not as an entry barrier.

1 · RoleFrontend, backend, full-stack, AI/agents, DevOps, or docs.
2 · GoalLearning, project work, open source, or team/guild.
3 · World SeedA starter world appears immediately.
4 · MissionsTwo or three fitting first missions.
5 · GitHubOptionally connect history and enrich the world.

9. Open Source as a Trust Anchor

The platform itself should be developed in the open so developers can trust, inspect, and extend its mechanics. Open source is also one world among many, not the product boundary.

Why open source?

  • Trust among developers
  • No black box for skill and reputation logic
  • Community-built worlds and integrations
  • A credible beginning in developer communities

Why not only open source?

  • Development also happens privately and commercially
  • Guilds and teams need protected spaces
  • Company worlds, sponsorship, and recruiting remain possible
  • The product can grow beyond contribution discovery
